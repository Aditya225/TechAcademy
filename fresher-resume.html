Why Freshers Get Rejected: 4 Real Mistakes Keeping You Unemployed (And How to Fix Them)

By Aditya Gaur · Published on September 2026 · 5 min read

Every week, thousands of engineering graduates step into the job market, submit dozens of applications, and wait for interview calls that never arrive. The most frustrating realization for entry-level candidates is often: "I have a degree, I know how to code, and I listed projects on my CV. Why am I getting ghosted?"

The reality of modern tech hiring is simple: engineering teams are not looking for applicants who merely know syntax — they are looking for engineers who can solve problems and demonstrate technical depth. If your applications are getting stuck in a loop of silent rejections, here are the 4 real execution mistakes you are making and the exact steps to fix them.

1. Relying on "Tutorial Clone" Projects

Having a GitHub repository link on your resume is standard practice. However, the contents inside that repository determine whether a Tech Lead keeps reading or moves to the next candidate.

If your portfolio consists of a basic To-Do list, a Weather App, or a cloned streaming UI built step-by-step from a YouTube video, it signals a lack of independent problem-solving effort.

How to Fix It:

  • Solve Real Problems: Build utility-focused applications (e.g., a real-time log analyzer, a rate-limiting middleware, an automated file parser, or a custom notification engine).
  • Write Production-Grade Documentation: Add a comprehensive README.md detailing the core problem, system architecture, setup instructions, and key technical decisions.
  • Provide Live Demos: Deploy your projects on hosting platforms (like Vercel, Render, or AWS) so recruiters can test them instantly without cloning the repo locally.

2. Describing Tasks Instead of Highlighting Technical Depth

A major flaw on entry-level CVs is writing generic, passive descriptions of responsibilities:

❌ Weak: "Created REST APIs using Node.js and MongoDB."
❌ Weak: "Worked on backend microservices."

These bullet points describe the tools you touched, but fail to demonstrate your engineering judgment, execution capability, or performance optimizations.

How to Fix It: Reframe every project bullet point using the Action + Technology + Impact structure:

✅ Stronger: "Engineered asynchronous message processing queues using NestJS and Redis, cutting API response latency by 35%."
✅ Stronger: "Implemented JWT authentication and role-based access control (RBAC) to secure multi-tenant system endpoints."

3. Asking for Opportunities Instead of Pitching Proof-of-Work

Clicking "Easy Apply" 100 times a day yields extremely low response rates. When freshers attempt direct outreach, they frequently fall into the trap of sending generic copy-pasted messages like: "Hi Sir, I am a fresher looking for a role. Please check my CV."

Recruiters receive dozens of identical requests daily. These messages offer zero context and force the recipient to do the heavy lifting of figuring out where you fit.

How to Fix It: Shift your messaging from asking for a favor to presenting proof-of-work:

"Hi [Name], I noticed your engineering team recently published an update regarding backend performance optimizations. I recently engineered a lightweight rate-limiting middleware using NestJS and Redis to handle high-traffic spikes [Link to Repo]. I’d love to get two minutes of your feedback on the architecture if you have a moment."

4. Treating LinkedIn as a Static Resume Instead of an Active Portfolio

Having a pristine CV while leaving your LinkedIn profile blank or unoptimized is a major missed opportunity. Hiring managers almost always inspect an applicant's digital presence before scheduling an initial screening call.

How to Fix It:

  • Optimize Your Headline: Replace generic student titles like "Student at [University]" with a clear value proposition: Backend Developer | NestJS, Redis, PostgreSQL | Building Scalable APIs.
  • Utilize the Featured Section: Pin your best GitHub repositories, system architecture diagrams, live demo URLs, and technical write-ups directly to the top of your profile.
