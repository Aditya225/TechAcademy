Why Career Guidance Matters Before Choosing Your Course or Career Path

By Techs Academy · Published on November 2026 · 6 min read

Your career is too important to be decided by guesswork, peer pressure, or last-minute decisions.

Career guidance should never be treated as just another business opportunity.

For every student, choosing a career is one of the most important decisions of their life. It is about understanding their interests, identifying their strengths, exploring opportunities, and finding the right direction.

At Techs Academy, we believe that career guidance is not about convincing students to purchase a course. It is about understanding their aspirations and helping them make informed decisions about their future.

The Problem: Students Often Start Planning Too Late

During our interactions with students from schools and colleges, we have noticed a common pattern.

Many students believe that career planning can wait.

  • Students in Class 10 start thinking about careers after selecting their streams.
  • Students in Class 11 and 12 are already occupied with their academic subjects and entrance examination preparation.
  • College students in their second or third year often believe they will start preparing for their careers in the final year.

But is waiting until the final semester really a good strategy?

The competition for opportunities is challenging, and companies increasingly expect candidates to demonstrate practical skills, relevant knowledge, and career clarity.

Starting career planning in the final semester can leave very little time to identify gaps, develop skills, work on projects, and prepare for interviews.

Career planning should be a continuous process, not a last-minute activity.

Why Do School Students Need Career Guidance?

One of the biggest challenges for school students is the number of available options.

After Class 10, students have to consider different streams, including:

  • Science (PCM/PCB)
  • Commerce
  • Humanities

But selecting a stream is only the beginning.

Students also need to understand what opportunities each stream can lead to, what subjects they will study, and which career paths align with their interests and abilities.

Similarly, students in Classes 11 and 12 often feel that career planning will add another burden to their already demanding academic schedules.

However, the purpose of career guidance is not to create additional pressure.

It is to help students understand their options early so that they can make better-informed decisions.

Why Should College Students Start Career Planning Early?

This is another important issue we frequently observe.

Many students join graduation or postgraduate programmes without fully understanding the career opportunities associated with their selected courses.

Some students later realise:

  • The course they selected does not match their interests.
  • They are struggling with subjects they never explored before admission.
  • They don't know which career opportunities are available after graduation.
  • They have not developed the practical skills required for their desired roles.

We have interacted with students who shared similar experiences:

"Sir, we selected this course in the beginning, but now we are finding it difficult to understand how to move forward."

These situations highlight why career guidance should begin before making important educational decisions.

Students in their first and second years of college have valuable time to explore their interests, understand industry expectations, develop relevant skills, and prepare for future opportunities.

Don't wait until your final year to start thinking about your career.

Career Guidance Is More Than Just Selecting a Course

At Techs Academy, we believe effective career guidance should focus on understanding the individual rather than simply recommending popular courses.

A student should not select a career just because their friends are choosing it or because a particular field is trending.

For example, today many students are interested in Artificial Intelligence.

But before deciding to pursue AI, they should understand:

  • What does a career in AI actually involve?
  • What foundational skills are required?
  • Which subjects and technologies should they learn?
  • Does this field align with their interests and abilities?
  • What learning path should they follow?

The same approach applies to Software Development, Cybersecurity, Data Analytics, and other professional fields.

The right career decision starts with the right questions.

How Techs Academy Approaches Career Guidance

At Techs Academy, our approach begins with understanding the student. We focus on:

1. Understanding Individual Interests

We try to understand what students enjoy learning, what motivates them, and what they want to achieve.

2. Identifying Strengths and Improvement Areas

Every student has different strengths and learning requirements. Identifying them helps us provide more meaningful guidance.

3. Exploring Career Opportunities

We help students understand different career paths, educational options, and the skills associated with their interests.

4. Creating a Personalized Roadmap

Based on the student's current stage and goals, we help them understand what they should focus on next.

5. Continuous Guidance and Mentorship

Career planning is not a one-time decision. Students may need guidance as their interests, knowledge, and goals develop.

A Message to Students and Parents

Don't wait until the last semester of college to start thinking about your career.

Don't select a course simply because your friends are choosing it.

And don't follow a trending career path without understanding whether it is right for you.

Whether you are studying in Class 10, 11, or 12, pursuing graduation, or planning your postgraduate education, the right guidance at the right stage can help you make more informed decisions.

At Techs Academy, we believe every student deserves an opportunity to understand their potential and explore the right direction.

Your career is not a race to follow others. It is a journey to discover what you are capable of achieving.
